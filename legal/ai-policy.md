## The short version

**No artificial intelligence runs inside {{APP_NAME}}.** Nothing you type is sent to a model.
No model runs on your phone. There is no chatbot, no AI companion, no automated analysis of
what you write, and nothing you enter is used to train anything.

**AI was used to write the app's text**, in the same way a word processor and a research
database were used: as a tool, by a person, who is responsible for the result. The section
"How the content was written" below says exactly what that means, including the part that is
uncomfortable.

---

## 1. There is no AI in the product

This is a statement about the software you install, and it is checkable rather than a promise.

- **Nothing you write leaves your device.** Not to a model, not to us, not to anyone. There is
  no network call in the app at all — see the privacy policy, and
  `__tests__/safety.test.mjs`, which fails the build if one is ever added.
- **No model runs on your phone either.** There is no bundled model file, no Core ML, no
  TensorFlow Lite, no on-device inference of any kind.
- **Nothing in the app is generated while you use it.** Every word you read was written before
  the app was built and ships inside it. The same person opening the same screen twice sees
  the same words. Nothing is personalised by a model.
- **No AI reads your journal.** The app does not summarise, score, classify, interpret or
  detect anything in what you write. What you type is stored, encrypted, and shown back to
  you. That is the whole of it.
- **Nothing you write trains anything.** There is no mechanism by which it could — your
  writing never reaches us.

The one thing the app does with what you write is arithmetic: it subtracts the minutes you
report today from the minutes you reported at the start, and shows you the difference. That is
subtraction, not intelligence, and the code that does it is `lib/reclaimed.ts`.

### Why this is stated so plainly

Mental-health apps are, at the time of writing, being rebuilt around chatbots. Some of them
are useful. Whatever their merits, they involve sending an account of your worst thoughts to
somebody else's server, and a person choosing an app for appearance anxiety deserves to know
which kind they have picked without reading a comparison chart.

## 2. How the content was written

**The text in {{APP_NAME}} — the twelve readings, the exercise scripts, the guided tracks, and
the copy on every screen — was drafted with the assistance of a large language model, directed
and edited by {{ENTITY_NAME}}, and checked against published clinical literature.**

That sentence is doing real work, so here is each part of it.

**"Drafted with the assistance of."** The prose was written by a person working with an AI
tool, not generated by pressing a button. Structure, clinical approach, what to include and
what to refuse were decided by a person. The AI helped write and rewrite the words.

**"Checked against published clinical literature."** The methods are not invented. They are
the standard components of cognitive behavioural therapy for body dysmorphic disorder —
exposure and response prevention, attention training, cognitive restructuring, behavioural
experiments, relapse prevention — as described in the peer-reviewed sources named on the
purchase screen and in `content/proof.ts`. Every claim in the app that states a number carries
its source. Claims without one do not ship; that is a rule the test suite enforces.

**What that does NOT mean.** It does not mean a clinician wrote it. It does not mean a
clinician approved it. It does not mean the app has been trialled — it has not, and the
purchase screen says so in those words, next to the research it cites.

### Why we tell you this

Two reasons, and only one of them is legal.

The legal one: describing where health-adjacent content came from is the kind of statement
regulators care about, and an app that let a reader assume a psychologist wrote it — by saying
nothing — would be trading on an impression it had not earned.

The other one is simpler. This app asks you to write down things you have probably not said
out loud. It is not in a position to be coy about its own provenance.

## 3. If that ever changes

Adding AI to this app would break most of the paragraphs above, and it is not a change that
could be made quietly.

Before any AI feature ships, all of the following happen first, in the same release:

1. **This document is rewritten**, and the privacy policy with it, to say exactly what is
   sent, to whom, and what they may do with it.
2. **The App Store privacy label changes.** "Data Not Collected" stops being true the moment
   anything you write reaches a third party.
3. **You are asked first**, in the app, in plain words, before anything you have written is
   sent anywhere — and declining leaves the rest of the app working.
4. **The tests change.** `__tests__/ai.test.mjs` and `__tests__/safety.test.mjs` fail on an AI
   SDK or a network call today. Someone has to delete an assertion deliberately, and that is
   visible in a diff.

A feature that requires four separate honest changes is not one that arrives by accident.

## 4. AI in how the business runs

Distinct from the product, and worth stating so the answer above cannot be read too narrowly.

- **Support email is answered by a person.** Nothing you send to
  {{CONTACT_EMAIL}} is answered by a bot.
- **No automated decision-making affects you.** There is no profiling, no scoring, no
  eligibility decision, and nothing that would engage the GDPR's Article 22 rights or the
  equivalent provisions in Quebec's Law 25. Access to paid features depends on one thing —
  whether a subscription is active — and nothing evaluates you.
- **The hardship grant is not assessed.** Tapping it grants access immediately. There is no
  form, no questions, and nothing, human or automated, judging whether you deserve it.

## 5. Regulatory position

Stated for completeness, and to be checked by counsel rather than relied on as it stands.

- **EU AI Act.** {{APP_NAME}} does not place an AI system on the market: there is no AI system
  in the product to place. Using an AI tool to author text does not make the resulting text an
  AI system, in the way that using a spellchecker does not. The transparency obligations that
  attach to AI-generated content are addressed by §2 above regardless of whether they are
  legally triggered here, because the honest disclosure and the compliant one are the same
  sentence.
- **Health claims.** No claim of treatment, cure, diagnosis or medical-device function is
  made anywhere. See `medical-disclaimer.md`, which is the operative document, and SAFETY.md
  §8, which bans the vocabulary in shipped prose and is enforced by tests.
- **Provenance.** Where the app states a number, it names a source. Where a method is
  described, it is a published method. Nothing is presented as a finding about {{APP_NAME}}
  itself, because there are none.

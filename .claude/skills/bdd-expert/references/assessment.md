# Assessment, Diagnosis, and Differential

## DSM-5 / DSM-5-TR criteria

BDD sits in the **Obsessive-Compulsive and Related Disorders** chapter (moved there in DSM-5 from Somatoform Disorders in DSM-IV — a substantive reclassification supported by the phenomenology, family-history data, genetic overlap with OCD symptoms, and treatment response profile).

**A.** Preoccupation with one or more perceived defects or flaws in physical appearance that are **not observable or appear slight to others**.

**B.** At some point during the course, the individual has performed **repetitive behaviours** (mirror checking, excessive grooming, skin picking, reassurance seeking) or **mental acts** (comparing one's appearance with that of others) in response to the appearance concerns.

**C.** The preoccupation causes **clinically significant distress or impairment** in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning.

**D.** The appearance preoccupation is **not better explained by concerns with body fat or weight** in an individual whose symptoms meet diagnostic criteria for an eating disorder.

### Specifiers

**Muscle dysmorphia** — preoccupation with the idea that one's body build is too small or insufficiently muscular. Apply it *even when other body areas are also a concern*. Associated with higher suicidality, more substance use, poorer quality of life.

**Insight** (this one matters clinically and is widely misunderstood):
- *With good or fair insight* — recognizes the beliefs are definitely or probably not true, or may or may not be true
- *With poor insight* — thinks the beliefs are probably true
- *With absent insight / delusional beliefs* — completely convinced the beliefs are true

The delusional variant is **coded as BDD with the absent-insight specifier, not as a psychotic disorder**. This is a meaningful change from DSM-IV, where it could be double-coded as delusional disorder, somatic type. Practical significance: **delusional BDD responds to SSRIs, not to antipsychotic monotherapy.** Roughly a third of patients fall at the delusional end. Poor insight is *not* a reason to withhold standard BDD treatment.

## ICD-11

BDD (code **6B21**) is likewise classified under **Obsessive-Compulsive or Related Disorders**, with broadly convergent criteria and its own insight qualifier (fair to good / poor to absent). The DSM-5 and ICD-11 conceptualizations are aligned; there is no meaningful practical divergence for most purposes.

## Screening instruments

### BDDQ — Body Dysmorphic Disorder Questionnaire (Phillips)

Brief self-report screener, the standard first-line instrument.

| Setting | Sensitivity | Specificity |
|---|---|---|
| Psychiatric outpatient (n=66) | 100% | 89% |
| Psychiatric inpatient (n=122) | 100% | 93% |
| Community sample (Swedish women) | 94% | 90% |
| BDDQ-DV (dermatology version) | 100% | 94.7% |

Variants: **BDDQ-AS** (Aesthetic Surgery) for cosmetic/rhinoplasty settings. Validated translations exist (Swedish, Persian, French, Arabic, Chinese).

**A screener is not a diagnosis.** High sensitivity with imperfect specificity means positive screens over-identify. Confirm with clinical interview. This is exactly why self-report-screener-based prevalence meta-analyses inflate rates (see `evidence-base.md` §1).

### DCQ — Dysmorphic Concern Questionnaire (Oosthuizen)

Seven items, dimensional rather than diagnostic. Useful for research and for measuring *degree* of dysmorphic concern in non-clinical samples. Does not map onto DSM criteria.

## Severity instruments

### BDD-YBOCS — the field standard

Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale **Modified for BDD**. 12 items, semi-structured, **rater-administered**, assesses severity over the past week across preoccupations, behaviours, insight, and avoidance.

Response thresholds — memorize these, they anchor every treatment trial in the literature:

- **≥30% reduction** in BDD-YBOCS total = **treatment response**; corresponds to at least "much improved" on CGI-I
- **≥50% reduction** corresponds to "very much improved"

When someone quotes a BDD trial result, the ≥30% criterion is almost certainly what "responder" means.

### BABS — Brown Assessment of Beliefs Scale

Measures **conviction, insight, and delusionality** dimensionally. The instrument behind the insight specifier. Use when the question is *how firmly held is this belief*, which drives prognosis and treatment framing.

## How to actually elicit BDD

The single most consequential clinical fact from `evidence-base.md` §8: **only ~15% of people with BDD have ever been diagnosed with it, and patients do not volunteer the symptoms.** Shame is the primary barrier; roughly a fifth said they would disclose only if asked directly.

So ask directly, and ask about **time, distress, and behaviour** — not about the feature.

Good questions:
- "Is there anything about your appearance you think about a lot, or wish you could change?"
- "How much time per day, adding it all up, do you spend thinking about it?" *(≥1 hour/day is the conventional threshold for clinical significance)*
- "Do you check mirrors, take photos, compare, groom, or ask people for reassurance about it? How often?"
- "Has it made you avoid anything — school, work, dating, photos, leaving the house?"
- "Have you sought or had cosmetic treatment for it? Are you planning to?"
- "Has it ever gotten bad enough that you've thought about hurting yourself?"

Bad questions: anything asking them to describe or display the flaw in detail, or that invites you to evaluate it. That converts the assessment into a reassurance transaction.

## Differential diagnosis

| Condition | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| **Eating disorders** | Preoccupation is with body fat/weight *and* the person meets ED criteria → Criterion D excludes BDD. But BDD and EDs genuinely co-occur, and non-weight concerns (skin, nose, hair) in someone with an ED can warrant both diagnoses. |
| **OCD** | Obsessions are not appearance-focused. High comorbidity (~34% lifetime) and shared phenomenology; the distinction is content, not form. |
| **Social anxiety disorder** | Fear of negative evaluation not anchored to a specific perceived physical defect. Usually *precedes* BDD when comorbid — and in BDD the social avoidance is downstream of the appearance belief. |
| **Major depression** | Low self-worth is diffuse, not organized around a specific bodily feature with associated rituals. Depression usually *follows* BDD onset. Misdiagnosis as "just depression" is one of the most common errors. |
| **Delusional disorder, somatic type** | No longer the right home for delusional BDD — use BDD with absent-insight specifier. |
| **Gender dysphoria** | Distress concerns incongruence between experienced gender and sex characteristics, not a perceived defect or flaw. Different construct entirely — do not conflate these. |
| **Olfactory reference disorder** | Preoccupation with emitting a foul odour. Separate ICD-11 diagnosis (6B22), phenomenologically adjacent. |
| **Normative appearance dissatisfaction** | Extremely common and *not* a disorder. Requires the Criterion C threshold: significant distress or functional impairment, with repetitive behaviours. Time spent (≥1 hr/day) and avoidance are the practical discriminators. |
| **Trichotillomania / excoriation disorder** | If hair pulling or skin picking is driven by an *appearance* concern, BDD is the diagnosis. If it is not appearance-motivated, the body-focused repetitive behaviour disorder is. |

## Screening in cosmetic and dermatology settings

Given ~20% prevalence in these settings and the 2.3% long-term BDD improvement rate after procedures, screening here has the highest yield of anywhere in medicine. Use the **BDDQ-DV** or **BDDQ-AS**. A positive screen is a reason for psychiatric referral before proceeding — not a reason to shame or dismiss the patient, which reliably drives them to a less scrupulous provider.

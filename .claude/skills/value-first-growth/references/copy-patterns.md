# Copy patterns

The words on the paywall are the product's character showing. In this category character is
the conversion lever, so this file is not a style guide — it is a strategy document about
sentences.

---

## The governing test

Read the line aloud imagining the customer is a friend who is having the worst month of
their year, sitting across the table from you. If you would not say it to their face in
that chair, it does not ship.

This single test catches confirmshaming, manufactured urgency, guilt notifications, and
most fabricated social proof, without needing a rule for each.

---

## Six substitutions

**Promise → arithmetic.**
Not *"Feel better in 12 weeks."*
Instead *"You said about three hours a day. That's 45 days a year."*
The second is checkable, theirs, and cannot be called a treatment claim.

**Feature → consequence.**
Not *"Unlimited thought records."*
Instead *"Keep taking thoughts apart after the first five."*
Nobody has ever wanted a record. They want the thought to stop running.

**Urgency → availability.**
Not *"Offer ends Sunday."*
Instead *"This price is the price. It isn't going up if you wait."*
Removing time pressure converts worse on the day and better on the year, and in this
category it is the difference between a brand people recommend and one they warn about.

**Testimonial → evidence.**
Not *"Rated 4.8 by 2,400 users"* when you have neither the ratings nor the users.
Instead cite the literature behind the method, and be exact about what was tested:
*"These exercises come from a protocol tested in randomised trials. Anneal is not therapy
and has not itself been trialled."*
Precision here is not a legal hedge. It is the reason the sentence is believable.

**Loss → continuity.**
Not *"Don't lose your progress."*
Instead *"Your history stays either way."*
Threatening someone's record with deletion to sell a subscription is hostage-taking, and it
teaches the customer the product is willing to hurt them.

**Command → invitation.**
Not *"Upgrade now."*
Instead *"Start my twelve weeks."*
Benefit-shaped calls to action outperform generic ones, and a first-person label reads as
the customer's decision rather than the product's demand.

## Naming the price

Say the number early, exactly, and without decoration.

- Lead with the annual figure and put the monthly equivalent next to it. The pair reads as
  honest; either alone reads as a trick.
- Never write a fake strikethrough. If nothing was ever charged at $99, $99 is not a price.
- If there is a trial, the sentence that follows the button states the date it ends, the
  amount that will be charged, and where to cancel. Three facts, one sentence, no asterisk.
- "Cancel any time in your app store settings" is not fine print. It is a selling point in
  this category and belongs at full body size.

## The dismiss

One dismiss. Present in the first frame. Same visual weight class as the accept. Labelled
with what it does.

`Not now` and `Close` are correct. `Maybe later` is acceptable.
`No thanks, I'll stay stuck` is a fireable offence.

---

## Notifications

A notification is a message from the product to someone who did not ask to hear from it
today. Three shapes are legitimate:

1. **A fact.** *"Your check-in is open."*
2. **A question.** *"How did today go?"*
3. **Something they asked for.** *"Your trial ends tomorrow, as you asked me to mention."*

Everything else — encouragement they didn't request, streak threats, "we miss you", guilt of
any kind — is the product borrowing against trust it has not earned.

Never fire a notification off a distress signal. Not after a bad mood score, not after a
crisis-screen visit, not after a missed week.

---

## Words to keep out of a distress product

Each of these is standard growth copy and each one lands wrong on someone who is already
running a harsh commentary about themselves:

`failed` · `missed` · `broken` · `behind` · `should have` · `only`
`don't lose` · `at risk` · `expiring` · `last chance` · `we miss you`
`unlock your best self` · `finally` · `transform`

`only` earns its own line. *"Only $6.67 a month"* tells the customer what the money should
mean to them. State the number and let them decide whether it is only.

## Two lines that do a lot of work

**On the free tier boundary, said on day one:**
> Week one is free, and grounding and crisis support are free forever. Everything else is
> part of Anneal+.

Stating the boundary early is the honest version of what a hard paywall does structurally —
it makes the commercial shape unmissable from the first session — without walling anybody
out. Customers who know the deal from the start convert better and refund less.

**On the ask, next to the proof:**
> You've seen your own number. Weeks two to twelve are how you change it.

Short, references something that already happened, promises nothing.
